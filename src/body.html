    How I built it
    1. Count possessions from the box score (Dean Oliver's estimate), averaged across both teams: FGA − ORB + TOV + 0.475·FTA
    2. Raw efficiency: points scored and allowed per 100 possessions, which takes pace out of it.
    3. Adjust for opponent: scale each game by the opponent's rating and repeat until the ratings stop moving. Home court is worth 1.4%, and late-season games count up to 1.5×.
    4. Rank by margin: AdjEM = adjusted offense − adjusted defense, the expected margin per 100 possessions against an average team.

    How I built it, and how well it works

    Compared within position. Every player is scored against his own group: guards, wings or bigs, based on position, height and how he plays (rebounding, shot blocking, three-point volume). A big's rebounds are compared to other bigs, a guard's assists to other guards. The first version compared everyone to the whole class, so bigs dominated: all four centers made its top 12, while only 3 of 24 guards made the top 15.

    Eight stats per group. Scoring and efficiency, rebounding, playmaking, steals plus blocks, ball security, team strength and usage, each as a z-score (how far above or below the average drafted player at his position).

    Age counts. Doing it at 19 is more impressive than doing it at 23, so younger players get a boost (about 29% of the score).

    Injuries don't sink anyone. Under 450 minutes, the player's previous college season is blended in. Jayden Quaintance played only 4 games before his injury, so his full 2024-25 season at Arizona State fills the gap. Players with blended seasons are never called steals or reaches.

    Score = ( 18·Z(pts/40) + 18·Z(TS%) + 12·Z(reb/40) + 12·Z(ast/40)
        + 14·Z(stl+blk/40) − 8·Z(tov/40) + 10·Z(team margin) + 8·Z(usage)
        + 40·Z(younger) ) / 140       Z = within position group (guards / wings / bigs)

    NFL · Receivers ·

    Is he actually good, or does his quarterback make him look good?

    Yards and catches mix a receiver's skill with his quarterback and his scheme. Player-tracking data lets me split it into two questions: how open does he get on his own, and does he gain more after the catch than the NFL's own model expects?

    What I found
    Unsung hero
    How the stats work

    Separation is the distance in yards to the nearest defender when the ball arrives. YAC over expected is yards after catch minus what the NFL's model predicts from where the catch happens and where the defenders are. Both come from Next Gen Stats tracking chips, pulled free through nflverse. Dashed lines are the averages of the players shown.

    NHL · Defensemen ·

    Who actually defends, not just scores?

    Points and plus-minus are what most fans see for defensemen, and both are noisy. Expected goals measure the quality of the chances. The real question: when he's on the ice at 5-on-5, does his team create more than it gives up?

    What I found
    Unsung hero
    How the stats work

    xGF/60 and xGA/60 are expected goals for and against per 60 minutes of 5-on-5 play with him on the ice, score- and venue-adjusted, from MoneyPuck. Per-60 rates let a third-pair guy and a 25-minute workhorse compare fairly. Above the diagonal, his team out-chances opponents when he plays. It's a team stat, so partners and usage matter.
